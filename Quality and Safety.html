Audit

Plan and perform clinical audits to evaluate care standards, identify gaps, and promote continuous quality improvement and patient safety.

What audits can we run?

Explore commonly used audit tools for your unit. Select, print, or run them digitally.

Medication Safety

High-alert medications, LASA storage, double-checks, fridge logs, and expiry reviews.

Hand Hygiene

Observe 5 Moments, ABHR availability, sink access, and promotional materials.

Documentation

Daily plan verification, consent forms, care plans, and pain/sedation scale completion.

Infection Control

PPE use, isolation signage, device care bundles, and cleaning/disinfection checks.

Falls & Pressure Injury

Risk assessments, prevention bundles, repositioning schedules, and heel protection.

Environment & Equipment

Crash cart seals, device checks, calibration status, and clutter control.

How to run an audit

1. Choose the tool & scope Select the audit type, area/unit, sample size, and date.
2. Plan the round Inform the charge nurse, prepare forms, and set a suitable time.
3. Observe / Review Use tracer methodology: interview, observe, and review 3–5 files or patients.
4. Rate findings Mark items as Fully Met / Partially Met / Not Met. Attach evidence if needed.
5. Create an Action Plan (CAPA) Assign a responsible person, due date, and target for improvement.
6. Close the loop Re-audit within 2–4 weeks and update your dashboard or KPI tracker.

Safety Event Reporting

Reporting safety events is an essential part of a learning culture. It helps identify risks, prevent harm, and continuously improve patient care.

🧩 Why Report?

To promote transparency, accountability, and patient safety by learning from incidents and preventing recurrence.

📋 What to Report?

Any event that could or did result in patient harm — such as medication errors, falls, equipment issues, or near misses.

🚨 How to Report?

Inform your charge nurse, complete the safety event form, and submit within the same shift.

Remember: reporting is not about blame — it’s about learning and improving care for our patients.
